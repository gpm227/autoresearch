The waterfall · where money comes from, in order

1

Weekly cash from day one

Every store pays 5% of gross revenues in royalty plus 1% Creative Fund (raisable to 2% on 90 days' notice), collected every Wednesday. Not monthly, not quarterly — the platform cash-flows 52 times a year.

Built-in raises at every renewal — the step-up waterfall

Legacy stores paying 4% must sign the then-current Franchise Agreement to renew — and the current form is 5% + 1%. Terms are 10 years with a single 5-year renewal on a 120-day notice window, so the renewal calendar is literally a schedule of contractual rate increases. Nothing to negotiate; the paper does it.

Why the brand is safe to buy

"Board & Brew" is federally registered and incontestable (reg. 5443521; §8/§15 declaration accepted October 2023). The marks are owned by affiliate JJ&N and licensed to BBRG royalty-free and exclusively, for 10-year terms renewable at BBRG's option, with "no significant limitations" disclosed. Because this is an equity purchase, the licensee entity never changes — on the disclosed terms, the license rides through the closing. Item 13 Item 1

The two conditions · say them out loud

Condition 1 · Two documents are not in the FDD

The FDD attaches only the Franchise Agreement and the ADA — not the BBRG operating agreement and not the JJ&N trademark license. The OA decides which closing structure we use; the license's change-of-control clause is the last open brand question. Funding closes after both are read — that's a condition, not a caveat. Item 22

Condition 2 · No performance representations

BBRG makes no Item 19 representations about store sales or profits — so unit economics come from the audited Exhibit B statements and store-level records in diligence, not from the FDD. We underwrite what we can verify, which is exactly how the Garts should want it. Item 19 Item 21

Two leaks, already locked

Lock 1 · The 99% store sale

A franchisee might sell 99% of their entity and argue the match right never triggered (the FDD's own summary invites the reading). It can't happen quietly: any equity transfer — controlling or not — needs our prior written consent with full deal papers up front, and an unapproved transfer is void and grounds for immediate termination. Permanent fix ships with renewals: the next form FA hangs the match right on any change of control. FA §12.2(a)–(b), (e) Item 17(n)

Lock 2 · The minorities shopping our offer

Bret & Mike (45%, and Managers) could take our blended offer to market as a price floor. Sequencing kills it: Close 1 first — Tom's 55% + JJ&N + the license — so any rival is bidding on a minority stake in a company we already control, while their two stores can't be sold past our 15-day match right. The offer itself goes out under NDA, no-shop, short fuse, non-assignable. FA §12.3 Item 2 Item 1
