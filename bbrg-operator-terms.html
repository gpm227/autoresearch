Gart only · proposed terms · Board & Brew platform

Operator terms: Greg and Kirk

Greg and Kirk sourced the transaction, hold the relationships it runs on, and operate the platform through the succession. The deal reaches Gart through those relationships; there is no process and no auction. The structure below is a founder package: Gart's capital is protected first, and the operators own a share of everything they build, the franchisor, the stores it acquires, and the real estate under them. Proposed ranges for the fee letter and holdco agreement. This document never reaches the seller side.

The structure

1

Diligence work fees

$20K per month, each, from the approved deal budget, payable monthly from letter signature. Expenses at cost. Work fees credit 100% against the closing fee. If a gate fails, fees stop with the budget.

2

Closing fee

2% of total consideration at each closing, paid at funding, less credited work fees. On the slow build it pays per closing as each one funds.

3

Management fee

The greater of $600K per year combined or 15% of royalty revenue, plus expenses, from the holdco. Below the market cost of replacing two full time senior operators running a $3M to $4M EBITDA franchisor. Any portion above the lender coverage covenant accrues rather than lapses. Covers the renewal calendar, franchisee relations alongside Tom, store acquisitions, lender reporting, and the amended FDD program.

4

Growth participation, paid annually

10% of royalty dollars above the FY25 base of $2.76M, paid each year once lender coverage clears. The same mechanics as Tom's earnout: computed from the weekly royalty stream, auditable to the dollar, and paid only on growth the operators created. This is the mid hold cash line; it pays through the years when the note to Tom consumes the distributions.

5

Common equity: 70% Gart, 30% operators

Holdco common splits 70% Gart, 30% operators, granted as profits interests at closing, no operator check. Gart also holds 100% of the preferred: staged capital plus a 6% simple preferred return. Distributions flow pro rata on common once coverage clears; at exit, Gart's capital and preferred return first, then common pro rata. The operators' equity vests through the succession and accelerates on a sale of the platform.

6

Real estate rides inside the same split

Real estate sits in the holdco, so the 30% applies to every site: leases assigned on transfers, properties that come with store buys, sites purchased when they pencil. Operators earn a 1% acquisition fee per property for sourcing and execution. Purchases above the consent threshold need Gart approval.

Roles

G

Greg · managing partner

Leads the transaction and the holdco: the economic grid, seller negotiations, the lender relationship, reporting to Gart, and the renewal calendar. Takes over operating leadership from about year three. Greg holds one permanent seat on the holdco board, held for as long as he holds equity, not subject to removal or vesting. Gart controls the balance of the board plus the consent list.

K

Kirk · chief operating officer, then expansion and real estate

COO for the first two to three years: the bottom up store audit, the per store schedule, franchisee support, and store operations through the transition. Hands operating leadership to Greg, then leads expansion and real estate: the site pipeline, property purchases, and the development program. Kirk also holds the founding relationships on both sides of the transaction and keeps them; he negotiates price with neither.

What the operators make · per person, even split, from the 8 year base case

A

Floor: the platform runs, the thesis stalls

Management fee floor, work and closing fees, no growth participation, equity worth little above the preferred. About $300K to $350K a year equivalent.

B

Base: the succession works

Royalties reach $5.5M by year eight; exit at 8.5x EBITDA, near $34M enterprise value. Cash along the way runs about $330K each in year one rising past $500K by year eight, from the fee, the growth participation, and pro rata distributions. At exit the 30% common returns about $2.6M each after Gart's capital and preferred, plus roughly $500K to $750K each of real estate equity. Roughly $6.5M to $7.5M each over the hold, about half of it cash along the way.

C

Home run: expansion and real estate compound

40 plus stores, royalties near $6M, a real estate book built site by site, exit at 9 to 11x. $9M to $10M each.

D

Downside: coverage breaks, capital impaired

Fees while they last, growth participation and equity at zero. Gart loses capital first.

Figures are per person on an even split, before taxes, from the 8 year model shared with Gart. The economic grid, lender terms, real estate pace, and hold period move them.

Guardrails

·

Capital first

Gart's preferred sits ahead of all common. Every operator cash line except the work fees gates on lender coverage. No fees on Gart's capital draws. Management fee above the coverage covenant accrues.

·

Gart consent rights

Acquisitions and site purchases above set thresholds, new debt, affiliate transactions, changes to the operator terms, and any sale of the platform. Everything touching the deal team prices at arm's length and gets documented.

·

Tax

Work fees, the management fee, and the growth participation are ordinary income. The common is granted as profits interests targeting capital gain at exit, held past the three year carry rule. Real estate gains are capital. Counsel papers the grants before closing.
