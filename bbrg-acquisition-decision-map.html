Diligence notes · justifies the action, or causes risk

Every reference below cites BBRG, LLC's actual Franchise Disclosure Document (Board & Brew, issuance date February 10, 2026) and its form Franchise Agreement. Each cite does exactly one of two jobs: justifies the action — the disclosure that makes the move at that box the right one — or causes risk — the obligation or defect the move creates. The FDD only summarizes; the underlying contracts control.

Gate 1 · Economics

Case + earnout, or no deal

Paper to pull
LOI with the earnout schedule; Item 21 / Exhibit B audited financials, FY2023–2025; Item 20 outlet tables.
Justifies the action
Item 21 attaches audited statements for fiscal 2023–2025 — the earnout base is provable, not projected. Item 20 shows a system that grew from 25 to 33 outlets over those three years with zero terminations and zero non-renewals — a royalty stream stable enough to pay a seller over time. The earnout structure is underwritten by the seller's own tables.
Causes risk
The same Item 20 tables show the opening pace decelerating: +4 outlets in 2023, +2 in 2024, +2 in 2025. An earnout priced off continued unit growth overpays. Price it off what the tables actually prove — the existing stores' royalties and the renewal step-ups — not the growth story.
Document pull · before diligence goes further

The two documents the deal turns on aren't in the FDD

Paper to pull · the exact request list
  1. BBRG operating agreement + all amendments and any member side or buy sell agreements. Read the transfer article and the manager voting rules.
  2. JJ&N to BBRG trademark license + amendments: term start, renewal mechanics, termination triggers, change of control, assignability.
  3. The JJ&N file. Tom owns 100% (confirmed), so one signature locks brand and control together. Still needed: tax status (C or S corp decides marks sale vs. license repaper), all marks and domains beyond reg. 5443521, UCC lien search on the IP, any direct licenses outside the franchise system, and the original Del Mar store's paper.
  4. All 26 executed FAs + amendments, side letters, renewal files. Output: per store schedule of royalty rate, expiry, renewal clause type, transfer clause.
  5. Affiliate store entities (SCBNB et al.): ownership of the 7 company stores and the paper tying them to the brand.
  6. The ADA book behind the $583K deferred revenue: development schedules and territory grants we would inherit.
  7. Money side: vendor rebate contracts (~$296K/yr), gift card terms ($392K liability), creative fund accounting ($353K liability), 2025 settlement file.
Justifies the action
Item 22 attaches exactly two contracts: the form Franchise Agreement and the ADA. The operating agreement and the JJ&N Trademark License are not exhibits — the FDD names them (Item 1: "JJN… owns the Board and Brew trademarks and has authorized us to license these marks"; Item 13 summarizes the license) but does not contain them. The two documents Gates 2 and 3 turn on are precisely the two the FDD doesn't carry.
Causes risk
This FDD speaks as of its issuance date — February 10, 2026. Any amendment, renewal, or side letter signed after that date isn't reflected, and pricing off Item 13's summary of a license nobody has read is exactly how the Gate 2 risk gets missed.
Gate 2 · Trademarks

Never BBRG without the marks

Paper to pull
The JJ&N Trademark License itself; USPTO reg. no. 5443521; the mark-grant clause in each store's FA.
Justifies the action
Item 13 discloses a stronger position than the gate assumed: "Board & Brew" has sat on the USPTO Principal Register since April 2018, with the §8/§15 combined declaration accepted October 2023 — the mark is incontestable. The license is "royalty-free," exclusive, runs 10 years "with us having the right to renew… for additional consecutive terms of 10 years," and Item 13 states there are "no significant limitations" on BBRG's right to use or license the marks. Because this is an equity purchase of BBRG, LLC — the licensee entity survives intact — on the disclosed terms the license should ride through the closing.
Causes risk
Item 13 also discloses the kill scenario in plain terms: if the Trademark License terminates, BBRG "would have the right to change the name," and franchisees keep the marks only "for not less than the existing term of their Franchise Agreements." Royalties survive to each store's expiry — then the platform dies at the renewals you were counting on for the 5% step. And the license document itself is not an FDD exhibit, so whether it is terminable on change of control cannot be verified from this document. The gate stands until the license paper says otherwise — or JJ&N sells the marks into the deal.
Gate 3 · Operating agreement

The fork that sets the closing structure

Paper to pull
The OA's transfer article — drag-along, tag-along, ROFR, blocked-transfer clauses — plus member consents.
Justifies the action
Item 17(j) clears the franchisee side entirely: "No restriction on our right to assign" (FA §12.1) — no store's consent is needed for any track. So the fork is decided by one document alone: the OA, which per Item 22 is not in the FDD. Item 2 names who sits at the table: Tom Powers (President/Manager of BBRG, President of JJ&N), with Bret Crutchfield and Michael DeSanti as the other two Managers — both also managers of SCBNB, the affiliate that holds company-owned stores.
Causes risk
FA §12.1's one condition: the assignee must be "willing and financially able to assume our obligations as franchisor" — a thinly-capitalized acquisition vehicle invites franchisee challenge on the only ground the FAs give them. And whichever track fires, Item 1 and Item 21 must be amended (new ownership, new owner financials) before new franchise sales resume.
Track A · one signing

Drag exists — cleanest disclosure story

Justifies the action
The OA's drag-along compels the minority into one deal — one closing, one Item 1/Item 21 amendment, shortest sales blackout. And the royalty step-up mechanism is papered: Item 17(c) requires a renewing franchisee to sign the then-current Renewal Franchise Agreement carrying "the Royalty… payable by new Board and Brew franchisees" — and the current form is already 5% + 1% Creative Fund (Items 5–6). The mechanism is contractual; the dates belong to each store's own agreement, some years out, so the raises are priced as upside rather than the underwriting base.
Causes risk
Item 17(b) makes renewal expressly non-automatic: one 5-year add-on, 120-day notice window, 90-day holdover cap, "no implied, notice-based, or course-of-dealing renewal rights." Every renewal is also the franchisee's exit option — push the step-up carelessly and Item 20's zero-termination table stops being true on your watch. And for legacy 4%-locked stores, their own FA sets the raise date: a current-form renewal clause steps them up at renewal; a same-terms clause defers it to final expiry. California adds 180 days' nonrenewal notice and a potential asset-buyback duty if renewal is refused (CA addenda) — the calendar, not the hammer.
Track B · control first

No drag, no block — minority on its own clock

Justifies the action
With OA transfers unrestricted, Close 1 on Tom's 55% + JJ&N + the license is lawful day one. Better: post-close you inherit FA §12.3 (Item 17(n)) — the franchisor's right to match "any legitimate offer" for a franchisee's business. Any partner store sale must come to us first under §12.3, so a third party cannot buy around us.
Causes risk
The match right has a seam: Item 17(n) advertises no ROFR on transfers of "less than 100% of the equity interest," while FA §12.2(a) attaches it to any controlling transfer — the 99% sale lives in that gap (Leak 1, below). And the blended offer itself can be shopped by the two minorities (Leak 2, below). Any minority fight becomes the next entry in a currently spotless Item 3, whose only disclosure is a 2012 JJ&N registration-violation notice. Keep it that way — it's in front of every prospect.
Track C · five-party close

Transfers blocked / ROFR — everyone signs or no one does

Justifies the action
When the OA blocks transfers or hangs a ROFR on them, a partial close isn't slow — it's void. The five parties are already identified in the FDD itself: Tom Powers, Bret Crutchfield, and Michael DeSanti (Item 2), JJ&N as mark owner (Item 1), and the buyer. Item 1 is also why Bret & Mike must be in the room early: the seven "company-owned" outlets are held by affiliates under common ownership — those stores belong to the partners' entities, not to BBRG, and don't come along automatically with the franchisor.
Causes risk
Every signature sits on the critical path — one holdout kills the whole closing, and the OA's ROFR notice periods set the clock whether you start them deliberately or not. Post-close the disclosure burden matches Track A (one Item 1/Item 21 amendment), but execution risk is the worst of the three tracks: the affiliate store entities (SCBNB et al.) each need their own purchase paper on the same day.

Capital and structure · how the money moves

Capital plan · two options, one destination

Slow build or fast close. The OA sets the menu

Option 1 · the slow build
Close 1 takes control and the brand on the small check: Tom's 55% plus JJ&N plus the license, conditioned on the OA handing operating control (manager appointment and removal) to the 55%, since three managers sit today and economics without the steering wheel is not control. Then the machine does the work: weekly royalties pay the paper, stores get bought at matched prices as real offers surface, renewals land on their own dates, measured new unit expansion runs on the prepaid ADA pipeline, and real estate accrues alongside through assigned leases, sites that come with store buys, and property purchased when it pencils. Bret and Mike come in on the same structure when ready, with a second Gart draw only after they sign. Slowest calendar, cheapest dollars, sellers get the installment and rollover treatment they want.
Option 2 · the fast close
Everyone signs the same day. Bigger outlay sooner, though still mostly lender money: the audited $2.6M of cash flow supports roughly $6M to $8M of senior debt at normal coverage, LC backed, with the note and earnout carrying the rest. The extra cash buys time and certainty: 100% and the marks day one. The income anchor under either option: BBRG distributed ~$2.5M to members last year (audited), putting Tom's 55% at roughly $1.4M a year if pro rata; the deal's payments beat that from year one while he keeps Del Mar. Drag along = we choose either option; blocked transfers = fast only; free transfers = slow available. Every dollar draws against a cross conditioned closing that cannot leave a partial state. And from the day the letter signs, Gart carries the deal budget: monthly work fees to the deal team plus legal, accounting, and searches, roughly $250K to $400K across 60 to 90 days, work fees paid monthly and credited against the closing fee if we fund. The team that finds the reason to walk has to be paid to look.
Seller structure · tax friendly by design

Tom's money arrives as capital gain, on his schedule

The structure
Equity sale of membership interests = long term capital gain (IRC §741). Earnout papered as contingent purchase price on the installment method, taxed as payments land, with stated AFR interest (§453, §483/§1274). Marks money, if JJ&N sells, is a fixed price with complete transfer since contingent trademark payments are ordinary income (§1253). Optional §721 rollover keeps him aligned and deferred.
The two rules that protect it
The earnout must never depend on Tom staying employed, or it becomes ordinary compensation plus payroll tax. And the buyer side pays for all of it: buying 100% of the interests is an asset purchase for tax (Rev. Rul. 99-6) with a step up amortized over 15 years (§197) against about $0.4M of book equity. Deal design points, not tax advice; tax counsel signs off before anything is signed.
The letter to Tom · what makes him sign

The LC funds a succession, not a buyout

The offer shape (opening ranges, locked at G1, re cut after the G2 document pull)
Tom's anchor is $20M and we pay it, structured. $4M to $5M cash at closing off the LC backed senior facility. $6M to $7M installment note over about seven years at stated AFR interest, roughly $1M a year. Founder's royalty earnout of $8M to $10M completing his $20M, paid as a share of royalty revenue above the FY25 base of $2.76M, capped, over a set span of years, so the price above the firm portion is funded by royalty dollars that do not exist yet. Plus the legacy package: Del Mar stays his, founder advisory role on separate paper, optional tax deferred rollover. The partners' 45% and their stores are priced separately through the blended offer.
What makes him say yes
The letter leads with the whole number, total consideration for the platform he built since 1979, with cash at close as a component inside it. The LC makes the day one check certain rather than conditional, which is what separates it from every broker letter he has fielded. His income never dips: from year one, note payments plus earnout meet or beat the roughly $1.4M BBRG pays him today, taxed as capital gain. The earnout reads as the founder's royalty, his participation in every renewal and store added while the system he seeded compounds. His royalty also makes him the transition's lead advocate with franchisees, since renewals drive his payday. The legacy terms cost nearly nothing and close the deal: he keeps the original Del Mar operation, takes a founder and advisory role on separate market rate paper, his people keep their seats, and an optional rollover leaves him owning a piece of what it becomes. In return the letter asks one thing: exclusivity covering the 55% and JJ&N together.

Leak points · noted to avoid

Two ways value walks out the side door — one through a drafting seam in the franchise agreements, one through the minority partners' hands. Each note names the leak, what the paper already gives you against it, and how to close it for good.

Leak 1 · The 99% store sale

A franchisee sells 99% and claims the match right never triggered

The leak
FA §12.3(a) scopes the ROFR to a transfer of the franchisee's "entire interest in the Franchise," and Item 17(n) summarizes it as no ROFR on "less than 100% of the equity interest of a franchisee entity." A seller reads that as: move 99%, keep 1%, and BBRG never gets to match. FA §12.2(a) reads broader — assignments are subject to the ROFR "except for any transfer of a non-controlling interest" — but the two sections don't say the same thing, and that ambiguity is the seam a seller's lawyer drives through.
What the paper already gives you
The consent wall holds even where the ROFR is arguable: §12.2(a)–(b) require prior written consent for any equity transfer, controlling or not, with the full deal papers and the buyer's franchisee application delivered up front — so a 99% sale can't happen quietly. An unapproved transfer "is void," is a material breach, and grounds for immediate termination (§12.2(e)). Consent is expressly non-cumulative, so a salami-sliced 50%-then-49% two-step needs fresh consent at every tranche. And the affiliate-entity escape (§12.5) only works into an entity with identical proportionate ownership — it can't smuggle in a new owner.
Close it
Condition every transfer consent on §12.2(c)(iv) — the assignee signs the then-current form FA — so each consented sale upgrades the paper. Then fix the drafting where you control it: in the next form FA, hang §12.3's ROFR on any change of control (cumulative >50%), harmonized with §12.2(a); every renewal (Item 17(c)) and every transfer forces the new form onto the system store by store. Conform the Item 17(n) summary in the next FDD so the disclosure document stops advertising the loophole.
Leak 2 · The shopped blended offer

Bret & Mike take your price to market as a stalking horse

The leak
In the base case — no drag, no block — the OA gives you no match right on the partners' 45%, so the blended offer (45% + their two stores) hands them a written price floor they are free to shop to a rival bidder. They're not outsiders doing it, either: Item 2 makes both of them Managers of BBRG with full system information. This is a Track B risk specifically — a drag (Track A) makes shopping moot, and an all-or-nothing OA (Track C) leaves nothing partial to shop.
What the paper already gives you
Their stores can't be shopped past you after Close 1: each store's FA §12.3 forces full written disclosure of any bona fide offer (§12.3(b)), gives the franchisor 15 days to take the deal itself on those exact terms (§12.3(c)), and the right refreshes if terms materially change or 60 days lapse (§12.3(d)). One verification first: if their two stores sit among the seven affiliate outlets with no FAs, the §12.3 lever is replaced by a sharper one — a store using the marks with no license needs paper from us the day after closing. Their 45% is defended by sequencing, not contract: after Close 1 you hold control, JJ&N's marks, and the license — a rival responding to the shopped offer is bidding on a minority stake in a company you already run. The stalking horse has nothing behind it.
Close it
Paper it anyway: deliver the blended offer under an NDA with a no-shop/standstill, a short-fuse expiry, and non-assignability, and condition it on a reciprocal ROFR over their BBRG units and store entities. Close 1 before the blended offer goes out — never the reverse order. And document everything: a Manager shopping confidential deal terms is a duty-of-loyalty problem you want on paper, not in court — litigation lands in Item 3, in front of every prospect.

Every track ends in the same three moves — renewal calendar, store buys, real estate — and each is anchored in the FDD: renewals run on Item 17(b)'s 120-day windows against 26 franchised outlets' expiry dates, store buys clear through §12.2/§12.3 approval and match rights, and the step to the current 5% + 1% form happens at each renewal under Item 17(c).
